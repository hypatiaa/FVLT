**The Farey spine is the rational skeleton; the helix family is its exponential lift.** This is the single sentence to carry forward. The construction has **three distinct objects** (§9.3a, do not conflate):

1. **Farey spine** $S = \{(0,0,z) : z \in \mathbb{R}\}$ — rational ordering axis carrying integer markers, Farey ticks, factorial-height markers. Projection target, not a member of the helix family.
2. **Helix family** $\{\Gamma_n\}_{n \geq 2}$ where $H_n(u) = ((1/n)\cos 2\pi u, (1/n)\sin 2\pi u, u)$. Each $\Gamma_n$ lies on cylinder $C_n$ of radius $1/n$.

**Lemma 9.3a (No Accidental Spatial Intersections, unconditional):** for distinct $m, n \geq 2$, $\Gamma_m \cap \Gamma_n = \emptyset$; for all $n \geq 2$, $\Gamma_n \cap S = \emptyset$. Cylinders are disjoint; helices share no points in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

## §9.7 Multi-prime divisor-lattice embedding (v2.7.13)

The §9.1a construction is single-cylinder: each $\Gamma_n$ on its own concentric axis-shared cylinder $C_n$, with bead $H_n(0) = (1/n, 0, 0)$ off the spine. To realize $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ as a geometric lattice for $N$ with multiple prime factors, multiple primes' helices must coexist while the unit, the multiplicative identity common to every prime tower, anchors on the spine.

§9.7 extends the construction via per-prime translation and rotation. The extension preserves the §9.1a per-helix shape — radius, phase periodicity, chord ratios — and intentionally abandons Lemma 9.3a's disjoint-cylinder regime: cylinders are no longer pairwise disjoint in this form. The trade is structurally apt. The unit, being shared by every prime tower, lives where all helices meet.

### Definition 9.7 — Multi-prime divisor-lattice embedding

**Status: definitional.**

For $N = \prod_{i=0}^{m-1} p_i^{a_i}$ under canonical prime ordering $p_0 < p_1 < \cdots < p_{m-1}$, let $n_i = a_i + 1$, $r_i = 1/n_i$, $\varphi_i = 2\pi i/m$.

The multi-prime embedding places divisor $d$, with exponent vector $(e_0, \ldots, e_{m-1})$, at
$$\mathbf{P}(d) = \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} r_i \left( \cos\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi e_i}{n_i} + \varphi_i\right) - \cos\varphi_i,\; \sin\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi e_i}{n_i} + \varphi_i\right) - \sin\varphi_i,\; e_i \right).$$

The $z$-coordinate is $\Omega(d) = \sum_i e_i$. The unit $1$ sits at the origin on the spine. The apex sits at exponent vector $(a_0, \ldots, a_{m-1})$ at height $\Omega_{\max} = \sum_i a_i$.

**Properties.** (i) Each prime $i$'s shifted helix passes through the spine at $z = 0$, with cylinder centered at $(-r_i \cos\varphi_i,\, -r_i \sin\varphi_i)$. (ii) Each cylinder has the open $\Omega$-spine as a tangent generator; distinct prime cylinders share the spine-contact locus by construction; no pairwise-disjointness claim is made in the translated multi-prime form. (iii) Mono-prime chains $\{1, p_i, p_i^2, \ldots, p_i^{a_i}\}$ trace prime $i$'s shifted helix. (iv) $\Omega$-grading is the open spine direction; cyclic structure is the $(x, y)$ direction; the helix family is the bundle relating them.

### Lemma 9.7a — Hasse–helix correspondence

**Status: unconditional.**

Each Hasse cover edge $d \to d \cdot p_i$ in $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ is the translated one-step chord of prime $i$'s shifted helix, with all other prime contributions held fixed. It advances $\Omega$ by $1$ and rotates the $i$-th contribution around its offset cylinder by $2\pi/n_i$. The mono-prime chain $1 \to p_i \to p_i^2 \to \cdots \to p_i^{a_i}$ is the untranslated chord polygon on prime $i$'s shifted helix.

> *The Hasse diagram is not overlaid on the helix family; it is the helix family read as a graph.*

### Definition 9.7b — Apex defect

**Status: definitional.**

Let $N$ have exponent-multiplicity profile $f: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{C}$ defined by $f(i) = (e^{-2\pi i/n_i} - 1)/n_i$. Under canonical prime phasing, the apex's complex displacement from the spine is $A(N) = \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} f(i) \cdot e^{2\pi i \cdot i/m}$. Equivalently, $A(N) = m \cdot \widehat{f}(-1)$, where $\widehat{f}$ is the discrete Fourier transform on $\mathbb{Z}/m$.

The **apex defect** is $\delta_{\mathrm{apex}}(N) = |A(N)|^2$, equivalently the squared $L^2$-norm of the first phase-weighted Fourier coefficient of the exponent-multiplicity profile, equivalently the squared distance of $\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{apex}(N))$ from the spine.

> *Apex defect is the Plancherel residual of multiplicity asymmetry under canonical prime phasing.*

### Lemma 9.7c — Cancellation law

**Status: unconditional.**

In particular, $\delta_{\mathrm{apex}}(N) = 0$ for every constant-multiplicity profile $a_i \equiv k$, since then $f$ is constant and orthogonal to the first nontrivial character of $\mathbb{Z}/m$. Squarefree $N$ is the canonical $k = 1$ case. Other nonconstant profiles may vanish by Fourier cancellation; these are phase-profile accidents rather than the structural default.

### Remark 9.7f — Basis/complement crystal congruence and joined objects

**Status: interpretive (structural identity); unconditional (poset-product fact).**

> *$\mathrm{Div}(30)$ and $\mathrm{Div}(1001)$ are congruent Boolean cubes: the basis cube over $\{2, 3, 5\}$ and the complement cube over $\{7, 11, 13\}$. Their geometric congruence follows from their shared squarefree three-prime multiplicity profile $(1,1,1)$.*

This grounds the basis/complement bridge of the FVLT $720/1001$ lens architecture as a congruence of crystals, not merely a pairing of numbers.

**Joined objects.** When $\gcd(N_1, N_2) = 1$, $\mathrm{Div}(N_1 N_2) \cong \mathrm{Div}(N_1) \times \mathrm{Div}(N_2)$ as graded posets (poset product; this is Rmk 2.2e of v2.7.10 read geometrically under §9.7). Under §9.7 with $m = m_1 + m_2$ primes, the embedded figure of $\mathrm{Div}(N_1 N_2)$ is the Minkowski sum of two sub-figures. Hasse cover edges separate cleanly by side: covers within basis primes and covers within complement primes never mix. In the $720720 = 720 \cdot 1001$ case, $\mathrm{Div}(720720)$ is embedded as an asymmetric basis body (profile $(4,2,1)$ on positions $0,1,2$) joined to a complement cube extension (profile $(1,1,1)$ on positions $3,4,5$).

**Strain non-additivity.** Apex defect does *not* split additively under poset join:
$$\delta_{\mathrm{apex}}(720720) = 1.864204 \;\neq\; 0.892582 + 0 = \delta_{\mathrm{apex}}(720) + \delta_{\mathrm{apex}}(1001).$$
The reason is structural. Constant-profile cancellation requires the prime phases to span a complete orthogonality system on $\mathbb{Z}/m$. At $m = 3$ in $\mathrm{Div}(1001)$ standalone, the three complement primes get all three cube roots of unity, and profile $(1,1,1)$ cancels. At $m = 6$ inside $\mathrm{Div}(720720)$, those same primes occupy positions $3, 4, 5$ with phases $(\pi, 4\pi/3, 5\pi/3)$, which are three of the six sixth roots of unity but not a complete orthogonality basis; the constant profile no longer cancels. The complement-side contribution at $m = 6$ is $1 + i\sqrt{3}$ with squared magnitude $4$. The basis side similarly reframes at the new phase stride. Both sides recombine to $\delta_{\mathrm{apex}}(720720) = 1.864204$.

> *Constant-profile cancellation is $m$-relative. A crystal that closes alone may not close inside a join: the join absorbs its primes into a larger orthogonality system in which the cancellation must be recomputed.*

The poset structure factors cleanly under coprime join; the embedded figure factors as Minkowski sum of reframed sub-figures; the strain does *not* factor additively. The join itself contributes structure — phase-redistribution strain — that neither side carries alone.

### Note 9.7i — Fiber-level non-crystallographic symmetry

**Status: interpretive (quasicrystal-analogy framing); unconditional (structural independence via 2×3 witness grid).**

Rmk 9.7e flags a quasicrystalline analogy at the *global* phase-skeleton level when the unlabeled $m$-fold skeleton lies outside the classical crystallographic range $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$. Rmk 9.7h exposes a complementary *local* phenomenon: even when the global phase skeleton is crystallographic, individual prime-power fibers may carry non-crystallographic polygonal symmetries. For example, at $m = 6$, a prime-power fiber with $n_i = 5$ appears as a pentagon inside a hexagonal global phasing — locally 5-fold inside a globally 6-fold skeleton.

This is a fiber-level quasicrystal *analogy*, not a crystallographic classification theorem. It is independent of apex defect: $\delta_{\mathrm{apex}}$ measures phase-weighted multiplicity-profile asymmetry (Def 9.7b, Lemma 9.7c), while fiber compatibility is governed by two distinct conditions on local fiber orders $n_i = a_i + 1$:

- **Local crystallographic status:** $n_i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ (classical crystallographic restriction orders in the plane).
- **Global compatibility:** $n_i \mid m$ (the local cyclic-group order embeds into the global $m$-fold phasing as a subgroup).

These conditions are distinct. For $m = 6$, global compatibility implies local crystallographic status (since $n_i \mid 6 \implies n_i \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\} \subset \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$), but the converse fails: $n_i = 4$ is locally crystallographic yet globally incompatible at $m = 6$ since $4 \nmid 6$ and $\gcd(4, 6) = 2$. A square fiber inside hexagonal global phasing is a structurally different incompatibility than a pentagon — the square shares 2-fold rotational symmetry with the hexagon, while the pentagon shares no rotational symmetry beyond the trivial.

### Remark 9.7j — Crystal-graph identification of Div(N)

**Status: unconditional (technical correspondence); interpretive (Kashiwara framework-bridge).**

For $N = \prod_{i=1}^{m} p_i^{a_i}$, $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ carries the structure of the **colored product crystal** of the tensor product
$$V_{a_1/2} \otimes V_{a_2/2} \otimes \cdots \otimes V_{a_m/2}$$
under the commuting action of $\mathfrak{sl}_2^{\oplus m}$ (the Levi subalgebra with one $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ factor per prime). Each prime contributes:

- a *crystal factor*: the length-$(a_i + 1)$ chain $|{-a_i/2}\rangle, |{-a_i/2 + 1}\rangle, \ldots, |a_i/2\rangle$, which is the crystal basis of the spin-$a_i/2$ irreducible representation of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$;
- a *weight identification*: the exponent $e_i \in \{0, 1, \ldots, a_i\}$ corresponds to the weight $m_i = e_i - a_i/2$;
- a pair of *Kashiwara operators* $\tilde{e}_{p_i}, \tilde{f}_{p_i}$ acting along the $i$-th weight string. Multiplication by $p_i$ is the coefficient-free raising operator $\tilde{e}_{p_i}$; division by $p_i$ is the coefficient-free lowering operator $\tilde{f}_{p_i}$.

The total $\Omega$-sum $\Omega(d) = \sum_i e_i(d)$ corresponds to the total weight $M(d) = \Omega(d) - \Omega_{\max}/2$. Divisor $1$ is the lowest-weight state, divisor $N$ is the highest-weight state, and $\Omega$-slices are fixed-$M$ subspaces of the product crystal.

This uses Kashiwara's framework (crystal bases, $\tilde{e}_i/\tilde{f}_i$ operators, crystal graphs, weight functions) only in the trivial-tensor-product case where the $m$ $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ actions commute. ### Remark 9.7k — Local-comb cardinality invariant

**Status: unconditional.**

Let $d \in \mathrm{Div}(N)$ with $N = \prod_{i=1}^{m} p_i^{a_i}$. The *local comb* at $d$ is the union of the $m$ prime strands through $d$:
$$\mathrm{comb}(d) \;=\; \bigcup_{i=1}^{m} \left\{\, d \cdot p_i^{\,k - e_i(d)} \;:\; 0 \leq k \leq a_i \,\right\}$$
where $e_i(d)$ is the $i$-th exponent of $d$. Each strand contributes $a_i + 1$ members; the selected divisor $d$ is shared by all $m$ strands, contributing $m - 1$ redundant counts. Hence:
$$|\mathrm{comb}(d)| \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_i + 1) - (m - 1) \;=\; 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_i \;=\; 1 + \Omega_{\max}(N).$$

The local-comb cardinality is **constant across selection**: every divisor in $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ has a local comb of size $1 + \Omega_{\max}$, with only the comb's *orientation* (which strand position $d$ occupies on each strand) varying by selected divisor. The multiset of strand orders $\{a_i + 1\}_{i=1}^{m}$ — the polygon-order multiset of Rmk 9.7h, visible as polygon side counts in the §9.7 top-down projection — is likewise invariant under selection.

> *Every divisor in $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ has the same local prime-action neighborhood cardinality, $1 + \Omega_{\max}$. The selection determines orientation, not size.*

In the crystal-graph language of Rmk 9.7j, the local comb is the orbit-closure of $d$ under the union of single-prime Kashiwara operator subgroups:
$$\mathrm{comb}(d) \;=\; \bigcup_{i=1}^{m} \langle \tilde{e}_{p_i},\, \tilde{f}_{p_i} \rangle \cdot d.$$
Its cardinality $1 + \Omega_{\max}$ is therefore the size of the single-factor-orbit closure for any basis vertex of the colored product crystal — a property of the product structure independent of the chosen vertex.

### Remark 9.7l — Superprimorial polygon signature

## ℚ⁺ Exponent Completion and the Balanced Throat (v3.2.2, extension block)

### Status and register ledger

**Extension block.** This is not ordinary $\mathrm{Div}(N)$. It is the **exponent completion** of the divisor lattice into the full positive-rational multiplicative group
$$\mathbb{Q}^+ \cong \bigoplus_p \mathbb{Z},$$
with one integer exponent coordinate for each prime. $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ remains the bounded nonnegative exponent box $0 \le e_p \le v_p(N)$, whereas $\mathbb{Q}^+$ allows $e_p \in \mathbb{Z}$. This block changes category from a bounded divisor poset to an integer-exponent free abelian group. Kernel companion: **Rmk II.5c**.

### Locked Core (unconditional)

Every positive rational has a unique integer-exponent vector
$$x = \prod_p p^{e_p}, \qquad e_p \in \mathbb{Z},$$
with finite support; this identifies $\mathbb{Q}^+$ with the free abelian group on the primes.

The reciprocal involution $x \mapsto x^{-1}$ is exactly exponent-vector negation:
$$(e_p)_p \mapsto (-e_p)_p.$$

The **extended $\Omega$-grade** is the grading homomorphism
$$\Omega(p/q) = \Omega(p) - \Omega(q) = \sum_p e_p \in \mathbb{Z} \qquad (\Omega(x^{-1}) = -\Omega(x)).$$

The **balanced throat** is the grade-zero subgroup
$$B = \ker\Omega = \left\{ \prod_p p^{e_p} : \sum_p e_p = 0 \right\}.$$
Negation sends the slice $\Omega = c$ to $\Omega = -c$, so $\Omega = 0$ is the unique grade-slice fixed setwise by the involution. Inside $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ the $\Omega = 0$ layer is only the unit $1$; inside $\mathbb{Q}^+$ it opens into a full balanced lattice.

### Minimal Witness: $2/3$ and $3/2$ (unconditional)

The first non-axis, non-unit balanced witnesses are
$$2/3 = 2^1 3^{-1}, \qquad 3/2 = 2^{-1} 3^1,$$
with grades $\Omega(2/3) = \Omega(3/2) = 0$ and exponent coordinates $(1,-1) \leftrightarrow (-1,1)$, swapped by reciprocal inversion. They are the smallest place where numerator and denominator both carry prime structure, so the reciprocal map becomes a genuine numerator–denominator exchange rather than an axis flip.

On the Farey / Stern–Brocot face, $2/3 = \frac{1+1}{2+1}$ is the mediant of $1/2$ and $1/1$, and $3/2 = \frac{1+2}{1+1}$ is the mediant of $1/1$ and $2/1$. All four adjacency checks are determinant-one:
$$\text{for } 1/2 \leftrightarrow 2/3 \leftrightarrow 1/1: \quad |1\cdot 3 - 2\cdot 2| = 1, \quad |2\cdot 1 - 3\cdot 1| = 1;$$
$$\text{for } 1/1 \leftrightarrow 3/2 \leftrightarrow 2/1: \quad |1\cdot 2 - 1\cdot 3| = 1, \quad |3\cdot 1 - 2\cdot 2| = 1.$$
So $2/3$ and $3/2$ are the first interior Stern–Brocot witnesses flanking unity.

### Unit-Fraction Spine and Farey Separation (unconditional)

For unit fractions $\frac{1}{N-k}$ and $\frac{1}{N}$ with $N > k \ge 1$, the Farey determinant is
$$\left| 1\cdot N - (N-k)\cdot 1 \right| = k.$$
They are Farey-adjacent exactly when $k = 1$. For $k > 1$ they are separated along the unit-fraction spine by the determinant-one chain
$$\frac1N \leftrightarrow \frac1{N-1} \leftrightarrow \cdots \leftrightarrow \frac1{N-k}.$$
Thus $k$ measures **Farey separation along the unit-fraction spine**, not Stern–Brocot generation depth. The endpoint mediant is
$$\operatorname{med}\!\left(\frac1{N-k}, \frac1N\right) = \frac{2}{2N-k},$$
a unit fraction $\frac{1}{N-k/2}$ iff $k$ is even (the $k=2$ case $\frac{2}{2N-2} = \frac{1}{N-1}$), not a unit fraction for odd $k$. The locked statement is **not** "numerator equals tree depth"; it is $k = $ Farey determinant separation along the unit-fraction spine.

### Audit-packet / witness-fixture discipline

**Status: engineering doctrine, tooling register.**

Applies to any cold-audit packet, replay bundle, or verifier fixture shipped between machines or models.

1. **Witnesses are pinned once, at lock.** Re-running a verifier confirms pinned values; it must never regenerate them. A run that rewrites its own expected values is regeneration, not verification. Repinning requires a new lock event with its own record, superseding rather than overwriting the prior lock.

2. **Per-file SHA-256 in the manifest, not archive-level only.** Per-file hashes localize which file drifted; an archive hash only reports that something did.

3. **No local provenance inside hashed fixture content.** Absolute paths, hostnames, wall-clock run timestamps, session identifiers, temporary directories, and other machine/session-local provenance do not enter any file whose hash is pinned. Content-relevant declared time values may appear only when they are part of the portable data being certified. Provenance that couples a fixture to one machine breaks byte-reproduction, which is the fixture's purpose.

4. **Portable content and local provenance travel separately.** The fixture carries what any auditor needs to reproduce the result; the manifest carries where and when it was made. Fields derivable only from local provenance, such as corpus directory structure, are declared null in the portable payload, never guessed.

Companion to the v3.2.3 standing rule: verifier runs certify computations, hashes certify content — neither certifies references or provenance.

## Substrate Layer (v2.7.7–v2.7.9)

**The substrate layer names what the divisor lattice rests on and what it does not contain.** Three remarks introduced across v2.7.7–v2.7.9 articulate distinctions that were already operational in trainer / instrument code but not yet kernel-explicit. These distinctions matter for any cold-start session, because they prevent the easy mistake of conflating *carrier usage* (transition history on a signed substrate) with *basis-change cost* (the factorial denominator that appears when discrete motion is read out in slope/calculus coordinates) — these are independent diagnostics, not aliases.

**Carrier vs lattice vs identity (Rmk 9.3e zero-clause, v2.7.7, definitional).** The signed affine / additive carrier and the positive divisor lattice are different objects:

- $0$ belongs to the carrier (additive origin / launch hub) but is *not* a node of any positive divisor lattice $\mathrm{Div}(N)$. primeFactors($0$) is undefined in the finite-divisor sense; $\mathrm{Div}(0)$ is not a finite positive divisor lattice.
- Landing at or leaving $0$ is a **carrier event**, not a Hasse move.
- "Zero is singular relative to positive divisor-lattice measurement, not singular absolutely" — Chris's softer formulation, preserved verbatim. Avoids the implication that $0$ is broken; in the carrier, $0$ is the additive origin.
- Identity symmetry: $1$ is the multiplicative root of every $\mathrm{Div}(N)$; $0$ is the additive hub of the carrier layer. Two roots, two layers, not interchangeable.

**Operational transition typology (Rmk 9.3f, v2.7.8, definitional).** For nontrivial transitions $n \neq n'$, three labels give a disjoint operational classification once the zero case is tested first:

1. **atom** — sign-preserving Hasse cover; single prime factor difference between $|n|$ and $|n'|$ (one $\div p$ or one $\times p$).
2. **jump** — polynomial-expression-required transition between nonzero values (multiplicative ratio is not a single prime, or a composite ratio that can't be expressed as a single Hasse cover, while no zero crossing occurs).
3. **zero** — carrier event with $n = 0$ or $n' = 0$.

Test order is explicit: **zero first, atom second, jump residual.**

The keystone "Operational" in the title is a scope guard. The remark is implementation vocabulary made explicit, **not** a mathematical partition theorem about all maps on $\mathbb{Z}$. Body-opening sentence preserves the procedural framing: *"for nontrivial transitions $n \neq n'$, the following three labels give a disjoint operational classification once the zero case is tested first."*

**Canonical routing as instrument policy.** Sign-changing targets *may* be routed via $\pm 1 \to 0 \to m'$ when the instrument elects to separate multiplicative descent from additive launch. Direct AP sign-changing jumps (e.g., $-3 \to 9$ via $d=12$, $k=1$) remain valid carrier jumps when explicitly declared. The $\pm 1 \to 0 \to m'$ route is an instrument choice, not a mathematical necessity; "canonical" must not be read as "compulsory."

**Difference–slope duality (Rmk 2.2a.1, v2.7.9, interpretive).** Forward differences and standard polynomial coefficients describe the same discrete motion in two different registers:

- **Difference register**: forward differences $\Delta f(x) = f(x+1) - f(x)$, integer-valued; **no denominator has yet been introduced**. The lattice register is fraction-free.
- **Slope register**: standard polynomial coordinates $\sum a_k x^k$; coefficients pick up factorial denominators when the same discrete motion is translated here. The slope/calculus register normalizes by run, which introduces $1/k!$ via the umbral correspondence.

Binomial basis makes the toll explicit: $\binom{x}{k} = (x)_k / k!$ where $(x)_k = x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)$ is the falling factorial — the discrete-calculus analogue of $x^k$ in the umbral correspondence $(x)_n \leftrightarrow x^n$. The denominator $k!$ is the factorial cost of expressing that discrete motion in standard polynomial coordinates.

**Factorial filtration $N_k = k!$ measures the basis-change cost** between the fraction-free lattice register and the slope-normalized calculus register. **Load-bearing sentence:** *"the denominator is not missing from the lattice register; it has not yet been introduced."*

**Carrier-usage independence scope guard (v2.7.9).** Factorial denominator is a basis-change cost, **NOT** a register-crossing diagnostic. Carrier usage is detected by the atom/jump/zero typology of Rmk 9.3f, **not** by the standard-form denominator. Two worked counterexamples settle this:

- $[12, 4, 2, 1]$ — pure-Hasse-atomic walk (every step is a single $\div p$ Hasse cover; zero carrier crossings). Standard form: $(1/6)(-5x^3 + 33x^2 - 76x + 72)$. Denominator $6$ despite zero carrier usage. (The additive step sizes of multiplicative descent scale with current magnitude, producing nontrivial polynomial curvature that requires basis-change cost to standardize.)
- $[6, 12, 30, 60]$ — contains a non-atomic jump ($12 \to 30$, ratio $5/2$). Standardizes to $6x^2 + 6$. Denominator $1$ via algebraic cancellation between binomial-basis terms.

Carrier usage is *transition history*; denominator clearance is *basis-change cost*; **the two diagnostics are largely independent**.

**Diagnostic architecture table (v2.7.9, attached under Rmk 2.2a.1).** Three independent diagnostic axes operating on the same trajectory:

| Axis | What it reads | Where measured |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| **History** | atom / jump / zero ribbon (transition types in sequence) | Per-step typology (Rmk 9.3f) |
| **Signature** | Newton interpolant degree (polynomial signature) | Newton readback of full sequence |
| **Translation cost** | standard-form denominator (basis-change cost) | Polynomial standardization output |

These three axes are independent. A signal is fully described only when all three are reported; reading any one for what another tracks is a register confusion.

**Lattice metric ↔ polynomial degree (Note 2.2a.2, v2.7.9, interpretive).** For descent-through-zero walks $n \to \text{atom}^* 1 \to \text{zero } 0 \to \text{jump } m'$ where atomic descent is a monotone shortest path through the Hasse diagram (one $\div p$ per step, $\Omega(n)$ steps total):

- visit count $= \Omega(n) + 3$
- Newton interpolant degree $\leq \Omega(n) + 2$
- standard-form denominator divides $(\Omega(n) + 2)!$

Identifies lattice metric $d(n, 1) = \Omega(n)$ (Cor 2.2b.1) with polynomial signature degree of descent-through-zero walks, modulo the $+2$ offset contributed by the zero event and carrier jump. **Each prime factor in the start contributes exactly one degree to the signature.**

**Two senses of shell (v2.7.9).** The factorial filtration $N_k = k!$ has two operationally distinct readings:

- **Lattice shell** — Div(720) hosting nodes up to $\Omega = 7$. Reads prime-support and multiplicity growth in the visited trajectory.
- **Polynomial shell** — factorial filtration rung $N_k = k!$ that the descent-through-zero walk's signature requires to standardize, $k \leq \Omega(n) + 2$. Reads denominator clearance in the polynomial readback.

The two senses are independent: a node $n = 720$ sits comfortably in the lattice shell at $\Omega = 7$, while its descent-through-zero walk would require polynomial shell $k = 9$ — overflowing $S_6$ in the polynomial register. Both index into $N_k = k!$, but they measure different objects.

**Unit floor and fraction boundary (Rmk 9.3g, v2.7.9, interpretive).** $1$ is the floor of multiplicative descent in two senses simultaneously:

1. **Multiplicative identity** — root of every $\mathrm{Div}(N)$; terminus of any monotone $\div p$ Hasse descent.
2. **Register boundary** — lower bound of the integer divisor lattice. Below $1$, continued division produces fractions, **exiting Div(N) into the slope register of Rmk 2.2a.1**.

This is why Rmk 9.3f routes $\pm 1 \to 0 \to m'$ as a zero event rather than continued multiplicative descent: from $1$, continued division would exit $\mathbb{Z}$ into $\mathbb{Q}$, exit Div(N) into the slope register. The composite three-stage route $|n| \to \cdots \to 1 \to 0 \to m'$ exhibits all three operational regimes in sequence: multiplicative-lattice descent (atom regime), boundary crossing (zero event), carrier launch (jump).

## Substrate-Level Architecture (v2.7.10–v2.7.11)

**The substrate-level pass names the trainer's two lattices and the two readouts each divisor channel supports.** Five remarks introduced across v2.7.10–v2.7.11 articulate distinctions that were operational in trainer / instrument code but not yet kernel-explicit. The patch separates the divisor lattice's role as *coordinate substrate* (carrying geometry, navigation, prime-exponent position) from a parallel *closure-of-description substrate* $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}$ that arises whenever observation-derived attributes accumulate (Rmk 1.5b), and names the second readout regime — supercharacter / Ramanujan harmonics — over the same divisor channels (Rmk 1.5c). The two substrates share the same primitive objects; the two readouts share the same divisor channels.

**Coordinate substrate vs. closure-of-description substrate (Rmk 1.5b, v2.7.10, interpretive).** Two lattices arise over the trainer's primitives: the coordinate substrate and the closure-of-description substrate.

The *coordinate substrate* is $\mathrm{Div}(N)$, with nodes as integer divisors, edges as single-prime Hasse covers, and geometry given by prime-exponent coordinates. $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ answers navigation questions: given a node $n$, what is its position, what nodes are adjacent, what is the structural relationship between $n$ and $m$.

The *closure-of-description substrate* arises whenever attributes are tracked over the trainer's primitive objects (nodes, visits, or transitions; cf. §1.6). Given object set $O$ and attribute set $\Sigma$ with incidence $I \subseteq O \times \Sigma$, the derivation operators

$$A' := \{\sigma \in \Sigma : \forall o \in A,\; oI\sigma\}, \qquad B' := \{o \in O : \forall \sigma \in B,\; oI\sigma\}$$

form a contravariant adjunction (Galois connection) between $(\mathcal{P}(O), \subseteq)$ and $(\mathcal{P}(\Sigma), \subseteq)$. Pairs $(A, B)$ with $A' = B$ and $B' = A$ are *closed descriptions*. The set of closed descriptions, ordered by extent inclusion, forms a complete lattice $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}(O, \Sigma, I)$. $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}$ answers closure questions: which collections of objects cohere under a shared description, which descriptions are forced once a subcollection is selected, which descriptions become available only after the lens expands.

**The two lattices share the same primitive objects but answer different questions.** $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}$ is not in general isomorphic to $\mathrm{Div}(N)$. The canonical case $\Sigma = \{\text{prime-power divisibility predicates over the join-irreducibles of } \mathrm{Div}(N)\}$ recovers $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ up to isomorphism, by the order-ideal representation theorem for finite distributive lattices [Bir40]. Once $\Sigma$ admits observation-derived attributes (walker history, lens state, atom/jump/zero classification), $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}$ diverges from $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ and provides genuinely new structure.

The trainer renders $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ as 3D geometry and reserves $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}$ as a semantic overlay over accumulated observations (§1.6). The naming follows the framework's principle of description over prescription.

**Same divisor, two readouts (Rmk 1.5c, v2.7.11, interpretive).** For each ambient $N$, the same divisor $q \mid N$ participates in two readout regimes.

In the *Hasse regime*, $q$ is a coordinate node of $\mathrm{Div}(N)$. Its role is navigational: it has a position in the prime-exponent lattice, Hasse-cover adjacencies, rank $\Omega(q)$, and lens-relative relations to the unit $1$ and apex $N$.

In the *harmonic regime*, $q$ is a supercharacter / Ramanujan channel. Its role is measurement: $q$ indexes the orbit-summed cyclic readout $c_q$ over $\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$ or over the relevant quotient / lens specified in §5.

Thus the divisor $q$ is not merely a containment object and not merely a frequency object. **It is a shared channel: its Hasse position says where the channel lives, while its supercharacter readout says what the channel measures.**

The two readouts are compatible with coprime decomposition. In the fused lens $N_{\text{FUSED}} = 720 \cdot 1001$, the fibered coordinate presentation $\mathrm{Div}(720720) \cong \mathrm{Div}(720) \times \mathrm{Div}(1001)$ (Rmk 2.2e) and the CRT decomposition of Ramanujan channels $c_{720720}(s) = c_{720}(s) \cdot c_{1001}(s)$ (Theorem 8.19) express the same basis–complement separation in the coordinate and harmonic registers respectively.

This remark introduces no new arithmetic claim; it records the architectural pairing between the coordinate material of §1 and the harmonic material of §5.

**Fibered presentation of coprime-decomposable lenses (Rmk 2.2e, v2.7.10, unconditional).** Let $N = A \cdot B$ with $\gcd(A, B) = 1$. Every divisor $n \mid N$ factors uniquely as $n = a \cdot b$ with $a \mid A$ and $b \mid B$, by unique prime decomposition. The map

$$\varphi : \mathrm{Div}(N) \to \mathrm{Div}(A) \times \mathrm{Div}(B), \qquad n \mapsto (\gcd(n, A),\; \gcd(n, B))$$

is an order isomorphism with inverse $(a, b) \mapsto a \cdot b$. The divisor lattice decomposes as the Cartesian product of partial orders:

$$\mathrm{Div}(N) \cong \mathrm{Div}(A) \times \mathrm{Div}(B)$$

with the corresponding Cartesian graph product on their Hasse diagrams. Cover edges in $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ are inherited from the factors: an $A$-direction cover $a \mapsto a \cdot p$ (for $p \mid A,\; p \nmid a$) or a $B$-direction cover $b \mapsto b \cdot q$ (for $q \mid B,\; q \nmid b$). No mixed-direction covers exist.

The 720/1001 bridge instance instance is the fused lens

$$N_{\text{FUSED}} := N_{\text{BASIS}} \cdot N_{\text{COMPLEMENT}} = 720 \cdot 1001 = 720720$$

giving $\mathrm{Div}(720720) \cong \mathrm{Div}(720) \times \mathrm{Div}(1001)$ with $|\mathrm{Div}(720720)| = 30 \cdot 8 = 240$. The trainer's fibered embedding renders this directly: each divisor $n = b \cdot c$ is placed at world coordinates determined by the basis exponents $(e_2, e_3, e_5)$ of $b$ within a basis-cell, with the cell itself indexed by the complement exponents $(e_7, e_{11}, e_{13})$ of $c$ across a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ super-arrangement. Every divisor of $N_{\text{FUSED}}$ occupies a unique geometric position; no projection from higher dimension is required.

The general statement extends to any pairwise coprime decomposition $N = \prod_i N_i$ with $\gcd(N_i, N_j) = 1$ for $i \neq j$: $\mathrm{Div}(N) \cong \prod_i \mathrm{Div}(N_i)$ as an iterated Cartesian product. The fused lens specializes to the canonical case (two factors) with the basis–complement split forced by the FVLT prime classification ($B = \{2, 3, 5\}$, $B' = \{7, 11, 13\}$).

## §1.6 Observation Architecture (v2.7.10)

This subsection formalizes the trainer's observation primitives and their relationship to the closure-of-description substrate $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}$ introduced in Rmk 1.5b.

**Prime-decomposition regime (Rmk 1.6a, v2.7.10, unconditional).** Trajectory context lowers the prime decomposition burden. Three regimes are operationally distinct.

*Cold prime decomposition*: a new ambient $N$ is factored from scratch. No prior structural knowledge. Trial division has cost $O(\sqrt N)$. For large unstructured $N$, the trainer does not assume cold prime decomposition is available; the user may supply a prime decomposition directly or construct the lens through a known process (factorial, lcm-of-range, axis-add expansion, prime-product specification).

*Warm prime decomposition*: within a known lens $\mathrm{Div}(N)$ with prime support $\pi(N) = \{p_1, \ldots, p_r\}$, a value $m \mid N$ has its exponent vector recovered by trial division against $\pi(N)$ only. Cost: $O(r \cdot \log m)$.

*Walk-relative prime decomposition*: a walker atom step from value $n$ to value $n' = n \cdot p^\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon \in \{-1, +1\}$) updates one coordinate of the exponent vector. No prime decomposition is performed. Cost: $O(1)$ per step.

The trainer's operational cost is dominated by warm and walk-relative regimes. Cold prime decomposition is invoked only at lattice construction (when the user supplies a new ambient $N$) or when the user supplies a target outside the current ambient's prime support. Within a walk inside a fixed lens, every transition is walk-relative; every divisibility query is warm.

This regime distinction is structural, not algorithmic: it states which prior knowledge is in scope for each query type. The direct-rendering cap on $N$ is a node-count cap ($\prod_i (a_i + 1) \leq N_{\text{render}}$) rather than a prime decomposition-cost cap. Lattices with structurally tractable construction sequences (factorial lenses, lcm-of-range lenses, axis-add expansions) reach values far past the direct-rendering cap without ever invoking cold prime decomposition.

**Three observation contexts (Rmk 1.6b, v2.7.10, interpretive).** Trainer-derived attributes attach at three distinct levels of primitive object. Each level supports its own closure-of-description substrate $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}$ (Rmk 1.5b). Conflating the levels yields invalid closure structure.

*Node context*: $O_{\text{node}} := \mathrm{Div}(N)$. Attributes describe intrinsic structural properties of $n$ — domain class (Def 2.2c), $\omega(n)$, pairCoords, lensStatus($L$)($n$), and any predicates definable over the lattice structure. Persistent across walks. $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}(O_{\text{node}}, \Sigma_{\text{node}}, I_{\text{node}})$ captures which sets of nodes cohere under shared structural descriptions.

*Visit context*: $O_{\text{visit}} :=$ ordered pairs $(k, n_k)$ drawn from a trajectory $T = (n_0, n_1, \ldots, n_T)$. Attributes describe per-event properties — was-target, was-revisit, lens-state-at-visit, $k$th-revisit-of-value. Specific to a single trajectory. $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}(O_{\text{visit}}, \Sigma_{\text{visit}}, I_{\text{visit}})$ captures which visits in $T$ cohere under shared per-event descriptions.

*Transition context*: $O_{\text{trans}} :=$ ordered triples $(k, n_k, n_{k+1})$. Attributes describe per-move properties — layer $\in \{\text{atom, jump, zero}\}$ (Rmk 9.3f), prime-used (when atom), polynomial expression (when jump), sign-flip (when zero). Specific to a single trajectory. $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}(O_{\text{trans}}, \Sigma_{\text{trans}}, I_{\text{trans}})$ captures which transitions cohere under shared motion descriptions.

The three contexts answer three different questions: (a) what structural classes of nodes exist; (b) what kinds of visit-events recur within a walk; (c) what kinds of moves recur within a walk. Each induces its own $\Lambda_{\text{desc}}$ when computed. Cross-context queries — e.g., "do nodes visited via jump steps form a closed node-class" — require constructing a fourth context whose objects span a chosen domain and whose attributes are pulled across levels, not merging existing ones.

The trainer's data structures preserve this separation: LatticeNode carries node-context attributes (computed at lattice construction), WalkerVisit carries visit-context attributes (populated during walking), WalkerTransition carries transition-context attributes (populated per step).

## Three Operational Transition Types (do not conflate, v2.7.8)

Per Rmk 9.3f. For nontrivial transitions $n \neq n'$, tested in this order:

- **zero** (tested first): carrier event with $n = 0$ or $n' = 0$. Belongs to the signed affine / additive carrier; not a Hasse move on the positive divisor lattice.
- **atom** (tested second): sign-preserving Hasse cover; single prime factor difference between $|n|$ and $|n'|$.
- **jump** (residual): polynomial-expression-required transition between nonzero values, neither atom nor zero.

"Operational" is the keystone. This is implementation vocabulary, not a partition theorem about $\mathbb{Z}$.

## Three Diagnostic Axes (do not conflate, v2.7.9)

Per the diagnostic architecture table under Rmk 2.2a.1. The same trajectory carries three independent readouts:

- **History** — atom/jump/zero ribbon (transition typology, Rmk 9.3f)
- **Signature** — Newton interpolant degree (polynomial signature)
- **Translation cost** — standard-form denominator (basis-change cost between difference and slope registers, Rmk 2.2a.1)

Carrier usage and denominator clearance are *largely independent*. Counterexamples settle this both ways: $[12,4,2,1]$ has zero carrier crossings and denominator $6$; $[6,12,30,60]$ has a non-atomic jump and denominator $1$.

## Two Senses of Shell (do not conflate, v2.7.9)

Per Note 2.2a.2:

- **Lattice shell** — Div($N_0$) hosting nodes up to $\Omega = $ multiplicity bound. Reads prime-support and multiplicity growth in the visited trajectory (lattice-progression panel).
- **Polynomial shell** — factorial filtration rung $N_k = k!$ that the descent-through-zero walk's signature requires to standardize. Reads denominator clearance in the polynomial readback (Newton-signature panel).

Both index into $N_k = k!$, but they measure different objects. A node $n = 720$ sits at $\Omega = 7$ (lattice shell) while its descent-through-zero walk would require polynomial shell $k = 9$ — overflowing $S_6$ in the polynomial register.

## Two Identity Roots (do not conflate, v2.7.7)

Per Rmk 9.3e:

- **$1$ (multiplicative identity)** — root of every $\mathrm{Div}(N)$; terminus of any monotone $\div p$ Hasse descent. Lives in the multiplicative lattice register.
- **$0$ (additive hub)** — origin / launch hub of the signed affine carrier. Lives in the carrier layer. Not a node of any positive divisor lattice. primeFactors($0$) is undefined in the finite-divisor sense.

Two roots, two layers, not interchangeable. Rmk 9.3g (v2.7.9) further names $1$ as the *register boundary* between the integer divisor lattice and the slope/calculus register: continued division below $1$ produces fractions, exiting Div(N).
