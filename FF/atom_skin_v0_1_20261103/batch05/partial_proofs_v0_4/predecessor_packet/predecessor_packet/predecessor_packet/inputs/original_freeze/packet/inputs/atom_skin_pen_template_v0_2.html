The pen turns to the next edge left or right and walks it. Beside it the same signs walk the universal cover, the regular tiling {4,ω(N)}. The skin

Div(N) for squarefree N with ω primes is the ω-cube: a divisor is a set of primes, an edge adds or removes one prime. Put the primes on seats in ascending order around a circle (2, 3, 5, … and back to 2). The skin keeps only the squares whose two primes sit on neighbouring seats. Every edge (prime p) then lies on exactly two squares, the ones with the two seat-neighbours of p, so the skin is a closed surface, and the squares around each divisor form one ω-cycle in seat order. It has 2ω vertices, ω·2ω−1 edges and ω·2ω−2 squares, so χ = 2ω−2(4 − ω) and the genus is 1 + (ω − 4)·2ω−3: the cube for 30, a torus for 210, then genus 5, 17 and 49. With square corners of π/2, each vertex has angle ω·π/2: below, equal to, or above 2π, so the curvature is positive, zero or negative, as in the curvature pen.

Drawing. The skin is drawn lifted: divisor d sits at height log d / log N, and its horizontal position is the sum of its primes' seat directions. Heights are distinct for distinct divisors, so no two vertices collide (flat seat projections of even ω put antipodal seats on top of each other). Drag to turn the atom.

The pen

The stream gives one sign per step: for Rule 30 and Rule 150 the squeezed-sphere readout st = σ(xt(0) ⊕ xt(1)) of the centre pair from a single black cell; for the coin a fair ±1 (seeded xorshift32). Arriving at a divisor along prime p, the pen takes the prime on the next seat to the left or right of p, as the sign says, in the orientation of the skin at that divisor (the orientation alternates with the number of primes in the divisor: this is what makes the skin orientable). Four equal signs walk once around a square. The pen starts at divisor 1 as if it had arrived along the first prime. On the cover the same signs drive the {4,ω} turtle of the curvature pen: turn by ζ2ω±(ω−2), walk one edge.

Flat atoms Div(pᵃqᵇ) (v0.2)

With two primes the seat circle has two seats, each the other's neighbour on both sides, so every square is kept and the atom is the whole (a+1) × (b+1) grid: the Boolean square for 6, a strip of two squares for 12, a 2 × 2 grid for 36. It is flat and CAT(0): corners have angle π/2, edge vertices π, inner vertices 2π. Being contractible, every loop on it is contractible, so the ledger reads ∂ events instead: steps where the edge the sign asks for leaves the grid. The pen starts at divisor 1 (a corner) or at the centre divisor p⌊a/2⌋q⌊b/2⌋, as if it had arrived along p going up. From divisor 1 the first sign of Rule 30 and of Rule 150 asks to leave the grid, so under Absorb both stop at step 1; start at the centre to see an exit time. Three rules:

Reflect. At a ∂ event the pen takes the other neighbour of its back edge, the one inside the grid, and the event is marked. Left and right keep their meaning (the stream is not mirrored), so this is not a billiard. Absorb. The walk stops at the first ∂ event; the ledger gives that exit time. Double. The grid is glued to a mirror copy along its whole boundary: a closed flat sphere, the pillowcase, with four cone points of angle π at the corners (curvature π each, 4π in all = 2π·χ). The pillowcase is the plane divided by the half-turns about the points (ia, jb), so the free plane pen projects onto it exactly with its turns intact: the cover panel shows that free pen, and the skin panel shows the pillowcase as a net, front sheet | back sheet (the back sheet drawn mirrored, so the middle line of the net is the side the two sheets share; the left and right ends of the net are glued, and the top edge and the bottom edge each fold onto themselves about their midpoints). At a corner both signs lead to the same edge, since the corner has only two.

Degenerate cases, labelled. On the Boolean square every vertex is a corner. Under Double every vertex is a cone point with two edges, and under Reflect every corner has one edge inside besides the back edge, so in both the walk does not depend on the stream at all: Rule 30, Rule 150 and the coin draw the same path (checked). On the 3 × 3 grid of 36 under Reflect started at the centre 6, the pen always runs centre → edge → corner → edge → centre, so it is back at 6 every fourth step whatever the stream; the signs only choose which edge and which corner (checked: 1024 returns in 4096 steps for all three streams).

In Reflect and Absorb the cover panel shows the same signs on the unbounded plane, for comparison: the flat Z² pen, where Rule 150 stays in |x| ≤ 3, |y| ≤ 2 for all time (Lean, FVLTS4sRule150PenV1).

The loop ledger

Each time the pen is back at divisor 1, two loops close: the excursion since the previous return and the whole path since the start. Each is classified:

■ homologically non-trivial: its edge chain is not the boundary of a sum of squares (exact: the equation ∂x = c is solved over the integers along the dual graph, each edge giving one difference equation). ■ essential, null-homologous: the chain is a boundary, but the lift to the cover does not close (a commutator-type loop). ■ contractible: the lift to the cover closes. ■ undetermined: the π₁ test lost precision (the excursion went too far out in the hyperbolic cover for floating point); the H₁ verdict stands.
