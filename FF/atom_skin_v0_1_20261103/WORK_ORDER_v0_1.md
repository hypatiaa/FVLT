## 3. Definitions (exact; indices mod ω, ω ≥ 3)

- **Vertices:** subsets v ⊆ Fin ω (v ↔ the divisor ∏_{i∈v} pᵢ).
- **Edges:** {v, v ∪ {d}} with d ∉ v (direction d).
- **Squares:** (v, i) with i ∉ v and i+1 ∉ v; corners v, v∪{i}, v∪{i+1}, v∪{i,i+1}. Only cyclically adjacent prime pairs give squares.
- **Orientation:** ε(v) = (−1)^|v|. Oriented boundary ∂(v,i) = ε(v)·( e(v,i) + e(v∪{i}, i+1) − e(v∪{i+1}, i) − e(v, i+1) ).
- **Pen step:** state (w, b), w a vertex, b the direction of the edge just walked; a sign s ∈ {+1, −1} gives d = b − s·ε(w) mod ω, then w ← w xor {d}, b ← d (template line 153).

## 4. Part A: statements to prove (all ω ≥ 3)

| # | Statement |
|---|---|
| A1 | Counts: V = 2^ω, E = ω·2^(ω−1), F = ω·2^(ω−2); hence χ = 2^(ω−2)·(4−ω). |
| A2 | Every edge lies in exactly two squares. |
| A3 | Link condition: at every vertex the ω squares containing it, ordered by i, form a single cycle of length ω (consecutive ones share exactly one edge at that vertex). With A2: the skin is a closed surface. |
| A4 | Orientability: on each edge the two squares' oriented boundaries carry opposite coefficients, so the sum of all oriented squares has zero boundary. |
| A5 | Genus: g := 1 − χ/2 evaluates to 1 + (ω−4)·2^(ω−3). A definition evaluated; no classification theorem is imported. |
| A6 | Pen well-defined: for every (w, b, s), d ≡ b ± 1 (mod ω), so the turn stays inside one of the two squares at w containing edge b; and s = +1 turns the same way relative to the orientation at every vertex. |
| A7 | Constant stream: a constant sign returns the pen to its start in exactly 4 steps, tracing one square's boundary. (Hand check: ε alternates along the walk, so d₂ = b, d₃ = d₁, d₄ = b.) |

## 6. Part C: the universal cover (owner decides)

**What it is.** At every vertex ω squares meet, each corner a quarter turn. So the angle sum is below, equal to, or above a full turn for ω = 3, ω = 4, ω ≥ 5: the cube (a sphere), the flat torus, and a hyperbolic surface. For ω ≥ 5, redrawing each square as a regular hyperbolic square with corner angle 2π/ω makes ω of them fit exactly around a point; these tile the hyperbolic plane as the regular tiling {4,ω}, which is the universal cover. The skin is that tiling divided by a torsion-free group of isometries, the image of π₁(skin). The skin matches Coxeter's regular map {4,ω | 4^(⌊ω/2⌋−1)} in the 2-skeleton of the ω-cube; its genus 1 + (ω−4)·2^(ω−3) is also the minimal genus of the ω-cube graph (Ringel; Beineke–Harary). One source check so far (arXiv 1211.4046, and the Springer abstract on polyhedral realizations); the construction should be matched against the source before anyone cites it.

**What the pen does with it now.** Each pen step multiplies an SU(1,1) matrix (one generator per turn, in the disk model). A walk "lifts closed" iff the product returns to ±identity, i.e. iff the loop is trivial in π₁. This is **floating point**: tolerance 10⁻⁷, marked undetermined above 10¹² (template line 120). Trivial in π₁ implies trivial in H₁, so the pen's "no closed lift with H₁ ≠ 0" is a consistency check, not a finding; the converse fails for commutator loops. H₁ is decided exactly (the dual-graph solve, using A4).

**Physics reading (saphir's question).** The SU(1,1) product is a holonomy: the transfer matrix of the walk. For ω = 4 the cover is the square lattice ℤ², the deck group is a crystal's translation group, and characters of π₁ are Bloch momenta. For ω ≥ 5 this is the setting of hyperbolic lattices (circuit QED {7,3}, Kollár–Fitzpatrick–Houck 2019) and hyperbolic band theory (Maciejko–Rayan 2021; non-abelian Bloch states 2022), built on the Fuchsian group of a {p,q} tiling. Abelian (U(1)) phases see only H₁; a non-abelian holonomy also sees commutator loops, which is exactly the H₁/π₁ gap above. Citations recalled, not checked.

**Options for the owner:**
- **C0.** No formal work; the cover stays drawing-tier evidence, read through classical covering theory.
- **C1 (exact, combinatorial; skeleton's recommendation if any).** Replace the floating test by an exact one. π₁ of the skin = edge loops modulo square relators. Every vertex link is an ω-cycle, so for ω ≥ 4 the square complex is nonpositively curved (Gromov's link condition) and for ω ≥ 5 hyperbolic; the word problem is decidable by normal forms / Dehn-type reduction. Lean target: a decision procedure for "this pen loop is null-homotopic", with a soundness proof and no floats.
- **C2 (geometric).** Realize {4,ω} in Mathlib's hyperbolic plane with explicit generators and prove the pen's matrix product is the deck transformation. Heavy; Mathlib's hyperbolic geometry is thin.
