Triangle pen

The dial is a triangle: three corners, each a part of a turn. The triangle fixes everything else. The surface comes from its corners, each turn is a root of unity at the corner the pen stands on, and each step is a side whose cosine is worked out from the corners exactly. The pen walks the tiling the triangle makes by reflecting itself. Curvature pen v0.4.1: the shaded start triangle now faces the right way on the sphere (it was painted over the front when it reached round the back). Earlier versions are kept.

The triangle

How the triangle decides everything

A corner written n has an angle of 1/(2n) of a turn: the root of unity ζ2n. Reflect the triangle in its sides, again and again, and the copies tile a surface. Around a corner n, exactly 2n copies meet. Which surface they tile is decided by one exact comparison of fractions:

1/nA + 1/nB + 1/nC > 1 : sphere,   = 1 : plane,   < 1 : hyperbolic plane

Each side's length comes from the three corners by the law of cosines for angles. For the side a opposite corner A:

cos a = (Re ζA + Re ζB · Re ζC) / (Im ζB · Im ζC)

On the disk cos becomes cosh, and on the plane only the ratios of the sides are fixed (they are the ratios of Im ζA, Im ζB, Im ζC). When one corner is 2, a quarter of a turn, the side between the other two corners p and q is half an edge of the regular tiling {p,q}, and the formula becomes cos(ℓ/2) = Re ζ2p / Im ζ2q: the {p,q} formula of v0.3 is the right-angled case of this one.

The pen

The pen walks the edges of the triangle tiling. Standing on a corner n, it sees the 2n edges spaced evenly. It turns to the edge next to the one it came along, on its left or right as the sign says, which is a turn of (n − 1)/(2n) of a turn, the root of unity ζ2nn−1. Then it walks that edge. The corners it stands on always go A, C, B, A, C, B, …, whatever the signs, so the turns and the step lengths repeat with period three, and only the side (left or right) comes from the stream. Three lefts walk once around a triangle.

Folded corners. A corner written n/k has an angle of k/(2n) of a turn. Around it the 2n triangles wrap k times, so the tiling covers its surface several times over: Schwarz's triangles (1873), whose tilings carry the star polyhedra. The pen then turns by (n − k)/(2n) of a turn. Not every choice of folded corners closes. Those that do are on Schwarz's list. For the others the flag machine stops at its cap and says so: a messy triangle.

What is drawn and how

Sphere. A finite flag machine: every (vertex, heading, corner) state the two moves reach is enumerated once, and the path is read from the table, so it cannot drift. The vertices are coloured by corner (A, B, C), and the start triangle (three lefts) is shaded. Plane. Exact: positions are integer sums of 24th roots of unity, reduced modulo the cyclotomic polynomial x8 − x4 + 1, so two positions are equal exactly when their eight integers agree. The sides are 1, √2 = ζ243 + ζ24−3 and √3 = ζ242 + ζ24−2, and 2. Disk. Products of SU(1,1) matrices; the view follows the pen. The corner the pen stands on cycles A, C, B, so there are three tiling patches, one seen from each kind of corner.

Playback, full screen with camera and pan, the step counter and ⇩ Run JSON (fvlt-pen-run/0.1) work as in v0.3.3. The video renderer render_pen_run_v0_1.py reads sphere and plane runs from this page. Disk runs are written with kind disk-triangle, because the pen's moves change with the corner, and that renderer does not draw them yet.

Status

classicalTriangle groups, the law of cosines for angles, the curvature split by the angle sum, Wythoff's construction and the {p,q} edge formula are classical (Schwarz; Coxeter, Regular Polytopes). Schwarz's list of spherical triangles with folded corners is classical (Schwarz 1873). exactThe surface choice is an exact comparison of fractions. Plane positions are exact in ℤ[ζ24]. finite / drawingThe sphere's flag machine snaps at 10⁻⁶, so its counts, the density and "did not close" are FINITE readouts, not proofs. Sphere and disk drawings are floating point. Nothing here is evidence about periodicity.
