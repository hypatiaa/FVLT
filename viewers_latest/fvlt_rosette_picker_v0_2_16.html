Wolfram used Rule 30 as a random number generator; FVLT's reference draws use the browser's secure random bits (crypto.getRandomValues, a cryptographic generator). Each roll makes four picks: the fair rosette pick and the naive rule (a spiral uniformly, then a divisor uniformly), each once with Rule 30 bits and once with secure bits. The first card asks directly whether Rule 30 and secure bits give the same distribution (two-sample chi-square). Uniform 1/M is the fair rosette law. A small p (say below 0.01) means the counts disagree with that law. For the naive rule its own exact law is the appropriate null model; uniform is not its law. A p of 0.01 or more means "not rejected in this run", not "equal". The p-values and the ±2σ bands assume independent, uniform-like draws; the summaries overlap and are not independent of one another, and each band is for one point, not for all points at once. Statistics are floats and are evidence only. Each card also gives the plug-in entropy of its picks; it runs low by about (M − 1)/(2T ln 2) bits for T rolls (Miller–Madow), so a gap of that size is expected. S is the spiral, N is the pick. q(d) = floor(M/d) is the number of spirals through d, and tau(s) is the number of divisors of s. Fair law: P(s, n) = 1/(M·q(n)) for n | s, s ≤ M. Reading the two directions: in D(X ‖ Y) the first argument is the data and the second is the predictor. D(PN ‖ U) scores the naive rule's picks with the fair predictor; D(U ‖ PN) scores fair picks with the naive rule as the predictor. Both are cross-entropy minus entropy. In general the two numbers differ; at M = 3 they happen to coincide (both exactly 1/3 bit).
Temperature: the divisor Boltzmann law d−β/ZN(β) is a softmax with logits −log d at temperature T = 1/β (FVLTDivisorBoltzmannV1.divisor_mass_eq_softmax); the fair rosette law is the zero-logit softmax on 1..M (FVLTRosetteLogLossV1.fair_rosette_eq_zero_logit_softmax), a different support from the β = 0 divisor gas.

Status

  • Proved in LeanThe fair law is uniform, so H(PF) = log M (FVLTDiceExactV1, in the library since v36+2, together with FVLTRosetteFairBridgeV1). The naive rule's pick is strictly below log M for M ≥ 2, and log M − H(PN) = KL(PN ‖ U) (FVLTRosetteEntropyV1). The four entropy modules FVLTRosetteEntropyV1, FVLTRosetteFairBridgeV1, FVLTDivisorBoltzmannV1 and FVLTRosetteLogLossV1 are published in library v36+3 (library SHA256SUMS 3db18131…, 527 entries; verified on nucleus, scout and membrane; independently audited). The first two were built by Codex.
  • Proved in LeanThe reverse direction D(U ‖ PN) is > 0 for M ≥ 2 (FVLTRosetteLogLossV1.fair_data_naive_predictor_pos); both orientations are cross-entropy minus entropy (crossEntropy_sub_entropy_eq_finiteKL). The value shown for the current M is computed here (float).
  • ClassicalThe chain rule H(N) = H(S) + H(N|S) − H(S|N), since H(S) + H(N|S) = H(S, N) = H(N) + H(S|N).
  • Computed hereThe numbers in the table: the exact laws above, evaluated in this browser, with logarithms as floats. The closed forms used: fair H(N) = log2 M, fair H(S|N) = (1/M) Σn log2 q(n), naive H(S) = log2 M, naive H(N|S) = (1/M) Σs log2 tau(s); the other entries are summed directly from the joint law, so the chain-rule line is a real check.

What is fair, what is structureThe facts behind the draw, the legend and reading notes.
  • Fair: s comes from your browser's secure generator (crypto.getRandomValues). Draws that would bias the result are discarded, so each s in 1..max has probability exactly 1/max.
  • Structure: the gearbox is an exact reading, not a source of randomness. Column s is read on the integer exponent e of the phase, and the contact rings come out as Div(s). A draw of s alone is a plain uniform draw.
  • Spiral mode: two stages, spiral first. Stage 1: a number n1 is drawn uniformly on 1..max and then one of its spirals k uniformly on 1..q(n1), where q(n) = floor(max/n) is the number of spirals through n; the spiral is s = k × n1 and n1 is discarded. This picks each spiral with exactly its fair weight. Stage 2: the number n on that spiral is drawn with integer weight Ls/q(n) among the divisors of s (Ls = lcm of their q), a fresh draw. The output law is identical to v0.2.3; only the work is lighter. P(n) = 1/max exactly. Draws are exact: 32-bit words from crypto.getRandomValues with rejection, big integers (BigInt) in stage 2 when needed.
  • Batch run: uses the same draws as a single roll, from the same buffered stream of secure 32-bit words (each word used once, rejection unchanged). The chi-square, z-score and p-values are floating-point statistics and are evidence only; the naive overlay is comparison only and never picks anything.
  • Exact: every decision uses integers. Floats are used only to place drawn points.
  • Rule 30 bits (v0.2.9, files from v0.2.14): with the dial on Rule 30, every word comes from Rule 30's centre column started from a single black cell, 32 bits at a time, most significant bit first, from one of two files: rule30_center_16777216.bin (224 = 16,777,216 bits, 2,097,152 bytes; computed on scout, its first 524,288 bytes are byte-identical to the smaller file, and an independent verifier recomputed all of its bits) or rule30_center_4194304.bin (222 = 4,194,304 bits; computed on nucleus, equal to a plain simulation on the first 3,000 bits and to Wolfram's CellularAutomaton[30, …] on the first 40). The page tries the larger file first, then the smaller, and checks the size and the first 40 bits of whichever it loads. The draw rules, rejection and BigInt stages are unchanged, so the fair and naive laws above are exactly what uniform bits would give; with Rule 30 the bits are deterministic, and the bars show how close Rule 30's centre column comes to those laws. That is evidence about the bits in the loaded file only: nothing here proves anything about Rule 30.
  • Entropy (v0.2.15): the section "Entropy (FVLT)" gives, for the current maximum, the entropies of the spiral S and the pick N under the fair law and under the naive rule, in bits, with the chain rule as a check. The fair pick carries the full log2 M bits (proved in Lean); the naive rule's pick falls short by exactly KL(PN ‖ uniform) (proved in Lean, FVLTRosetteEntropyV1, published in library v36+3). After a batch run, the plug-in entropy of the observed picks is shown against log2 M; it runs low by about (M − 1)/(2T ln 2) bits for T rolls (Miller–Madow), so a gap of that size is expected. The run is evidence only.
  • Library status and reverse direction (v0.2.16): the four entropy modules (FVLTRosetteEntropyV1, FVLTRosetteFairBridgeV1, FVLTDivisorBoltzmannV1, FVLTRosetteLogLossV1) are published in library v36+3, and FVLTDiceExactV1 since v36+2. The Entropy section also shows D(U ‖ PN), fair data scored by the naive rule as predictor, next to D(PN ‖ U); the first argument is the data, the second the predictor, and in general the two numbers differ (M = 100: 1.2655 and 1.7451 bits; at M = 3 both are exactly 1/3 bit). Proved in Lean: D(U ‖ PN) > 0 for M ≥ 2. One line reads the divisor Boltzmann law and the fair rosette law as softmax laws (temperature T = 1/β).
