What is drawn

At each time t take the centre pair (q, r) = (xt(0), xt(1)) and place it on the pair torus at (θq, θr) with white ↦ 1 and black ↦ −1 = ζ2 (half a turn). Squeezing the torus to the sphere (major radius 0) sends it to (r·σqσr, 0, 0), so the sphere sees one sign st = σqσr = σ(q ⊕ r). The pen turns by st·θ (left on +, right on −) and then walks a geodesic arc α. Red dots on the Rule 30 path mark the tooth: q = r = 1, the corner the squeeze glues to white, where Rule 30's OR differs from XOR.

Control. The green sphere is driven by a fair coin instead of a rule: each sign is ±1 with probability ½, from a seeded pseudo-random generator (xorshift32, seed shown; "New seed" draws one from the browser's crypto source). If the Rule 30 picture looks like the coin picture, its "continents" are a picture of randomness, not of something specific to Rule 30. Coverage counts the fraction of 432 equal-area cells (24 bands of equal height in z × 18 longitude sectors) the pen has entered. It is a crude summary, not a dimension estimate.

The general formula (v0.1.10)

Every preset is one case of a single formula for the regular tiling {p,q}: the edge ℓ satisfies cos(ℓ/2) = Re ζ2p / Im ζ2q, and the turn is ζ2q±(q−2). The table above follows from it. The curvature pen v0.3.0 carries the same formula to the plane and the hyperbolic plane with a {p,q} dial.

Watching the pen walk (v0.1.7)

Play now moves the pen smoothly along each edge at the chosen speed, from one step every five seconds up to twenty steps a second; it starts at one step a second, and the − Slower and Faster + buttons beside Play change it while it walks. While it walks, the full solid is dimmed, the path it has taken is highlighted, the last six steps glow brightly, and a red ring flashes when a tooth event (q = r = 1) happens. Pause holds the picture, Step ▸ advances one step, and Restart goes back to step 0. Changing any setting leaves playback and shows the whole path again.

Platonic presets

Project a Platonic solid {p,q} radially onto the sphere. Its edges become great-circle arcs of one length α, and the q edges at a vertex meet at equal angles, each 1/q of a turn. A pen arriving along one edge finds the other edges at turns of ±(q − 2j)/(2q) of a turn from straight ahead: the root of unity ζ2qq−2j. So a ± turn by that root of unity, with step α equal to the edge arc, keeps the pen on the solid's skeleton whatever the sign stream says.

Only the octahedron's step is a root of unity (i = ζ4). The other four steps are unit complex numbers in ℚ(i, √2) or ℚ(i, √5) that are not roots of unity (Niven's theorem), so no grid of ζN steps can reach them: that is why grid settings near them drift.

The slider grid has α in 96ths and θ in 24ths of a turn, so only the octahedron sits on it exactly; the others are near grid points, and on the grid they drift off the skeleton over time. A preset uses the exact angles. The page also turns the pen into a finite flag machine: it enumerates every (vertex, heading) state the two moves can reach and then follows the table, so the path cannot drift. The faint lines are the solid's skeleton. Each card reports the vertices and edges its pen has visited, and the number of machine states reachable from the start.

Messy presets (from v0.1.3) reproduce the grid settings saphir found by hand: the cube with step ζ9619 (cos ≈ 0.3214 against the edge's exact 1/3, so slightly too long) and the dodecahedron with step ζ9611 (cos ≈ 0.7518 against √5/3 ≈ 0.7454, slightly too short), both turning by ζ6. Each step misses the vertex slightly, the error builds, and the drawn solid slowly precesses. The exact solid is drawn faint behind, so the drift shows against it.

Screenshot presets (from v0.1.4) restore the exact settings of four screenshots saphir took on 2026-10-31: slider positions, step count T and coin seed. Each should reproduce the sign counts shown in the screenshot.

Full screen (this version)

Every view has a ⛶ Full screen button in its top corner. In full screen the view fills the screen with its own controls: − Slower, Play, Faster +, Step ▸, Restart and the current speed, plus camera controls. Spheres turn with ⟲ ⟳ ▲ ▼ or by dragging; every view moves with ◀ ▶ ▲ ▼ or a two-finger drag (flat views also by a one-finger drag); every view zooms with − and +, a two-finger pinch, or the mouse wheel. Reset camera returns the view to its start, and ✕ Exit (or Escape) leaves full screen.

Hamilton's icosian word (v0.1.14)

In 1857 W. R. Hamilton posed the Icosian game, a round trip along the dodecahedron's edges through all 20 vertices exactly once (a Hamiltonian cycle), and solved it with his icosian calculus: two moves, turn left and turn right, whose group is A5. Those are this pen's two moves. His word L L L R R R L R L R, taken twice, is a Hamiltonian cycle; the fourth card plays it, repeated, beside Rule 150, Rule 30 and the coin. On the dodecahedron the card checks the cycle: all 20 vertices once, back at the start at step 20. On other solids the same word is just a periodic sign stream. Every card on an exact preset now also shows its cover times: the first step at which all vertices, and all edges, have been reached. The Icosian game preset is the dodecahedron with 20 steps.

Hamiltonian cycles are where the travelling salesman problem starts: on a solid whose edges all have the same length, the shortest round trip through every vertex is exactly a Hamiltonian cycle. Covering every edge is the Chinese postman problem. Nothing here bears on how hard those problems are in general.

Export a run (fvlt-pen-run/0.1)

Every view has a ⇩ Run JSON button, on the page and in full screen. It saves the run as it is now: the stream (signs, seed, tooth events), the rule and its exact labels, the geometry (skeleton, lattice or tiling patch), the path for every step, the camera, the play speed, and statistics for every step (vertices, edges, tooth events, distance) with final totals. The renderer pen_tools/render_pen_run_v0_1.py turns such a file into a video like the Platonic-solid videos.
