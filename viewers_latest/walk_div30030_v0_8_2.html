Why the lift is exact. The skin of a squarefree atom with w primes is built from squares on neighbouring seat pairs (i, i+1 mod w). Every vertex lies on w squares, so w right angles meet there. Its universal cover is the tiling by squares with w at each corner, {4,w}, whose dual is the tiling by right-angled regular w-gons, {w,4}. The tiles of {w,4} are the elements of the right-angled reflection group Ww. A tile's parity vector (which reflections occur an odd number of times) is a divisor of N, and that is the covering map. A pen step that flips seat d lifts to g → g·rd. The page tracks g as a reduced word, so every count on the right is exact.

Curvature. All angles in turns. Each square corner is a quarter turn, so w squares meet in w/4 turn: w = 4 is flat (1 turn), w = 5 and 6 are hyperbolic (5/4 and 3/2 turns), w = 3 is spherical (3/4 turn). Gauss–Bonnet in turns: the deficits (1 − edges/4) add up to χ, i.e. Σ(4 − edges) = 4χ in whole numbers. So χ = 0, −8, −32 for 210, 2310 and 30030 (genus 1, 5 and 17), and χ = 2 for 720 (a sphere).

Revisits. When the pen returns to a divisor it has visited before, the lift either returns to a tile it has visited (a loop that closes in the cover, green) or lands on a new tile carrying the same divisor (the loop closes only because the surface is folded, orange).

Strained atoms (720, 720720). The skin is the squarefree skin with every square on seats (i, i+1) cut into ai × ai+1 small squares (profile_skin_v0_1). Cutting does not change the surface, so the cover is the same: the {4,6} tiling for 720720, the sphere itself for 720. A point of the lift is (tile, exponent vector); the tile changes by one reflection exactly when a coordinate reaches its top value or leaves it. In the cone layout, gcd jumps are drawn along the cone surface.

Pillow walks (two primes). For Div(2a3b) the skin is the a × b rectangle of divisors doubled into a pillowcase, as in the atom viewer's default ("Double"). The pen walks the plane, turning a quarter turn every step (Rule 30 picks left or right), and the plane folds onto the pillowcase by half turns about the points (ka, lb). The four corners 1, 2a, 3b, N are cone points of half a turn; their deficits, half a turn each, add up to 2 = χ (a sphere). The unfolded plane walk is the same for every pillow atom, and it is also the lift of Div(210)'s flat torus; only the folding differs.

Curvature ladder. The lift panel uses one construction for every atom: the right-angled reflection group of the seats, with each tile metrized as the all-right regular w-gon. w = 3 (Div(720)) gives octants of the round sphere, CAT(1); w = 4 (Div(210)) flat squares, CAT(0); w = 5, 6 (2310, 30030, 720720) right-angled hyperbolic pentagons and hexagons, CAT(−1). The flat-square skin of Div(720) itself is not CAT(1): around each of its 8 corners the total angle is 3/4 turn, so its link is shorter than one full turn, which CAT(1) forbids.

Moving through the lift (v0.7.4). The view is held as a tile of the cover, written as an exact word from the pen's tile, plus a small map inside that tile; after every drag the anchor moves to the tile nearest the centre, so travel is unlimited. The pen and its trail are placed by exact words and drawn within 14 walls of the view (farther points sit on the rim anyway; a note says how far away the pen is). Earlier: In the lift panel, drag moves through the geometry itself: a hyperbolic translation in the Poincaré disk, a translation of the plane, a rotation of the sphere (ball view: drag turns the ball). The disk stays the disk and the tiles flow. Choose "pan the picture" (or hold shift) to slide the picture instead; reset returns to the pen.

Zoom and pan. Mouse wheel or two-finger pinch zooms, drag pans (on the atom, drag rotates unless "drag: pan" is chosen or shift is held), reset restores the view. Zoom and pan only move the picture; no data or check depends on them.

Drawing choices. In the helical layout one step flips one wheel, drawn as a half-circle of radius 1/2 (teeth move linearly, no wrap). The cone and the straight-edge helical layout draw steps as straight chords between the exact vertex positions. Tile edges in the lift are drawn as chords and the path as geodesics. The lift's frame turns with the pen. All of this is drawing only; every count comes from the exact paths and words.
