From one orbit to a Ramanujan sum

At frequency y = 1 the selected orbit contributes σX(1) = Σx∈X exp(2πix/n), with each distinct residue counted once. The units modulo n split into cosets of ⟨a⟩. Adding one sum from every unit coset gives the full Ramanujan sum cn(1) = μ(n).

Try n = 7 and a = 2. The two unit orbits give conjugate sums (−1 + i√7)/2 and (−1 − i√7)/2. Together they give c7(1) = −1 exactly.

Open the same q = 7 row in the GENESIS gcd-shell viewer. Its shell gcd(x, N) = d sums to cN/d(y) at integer frequency y. The example uses N = 210, d = 30, and y = 1. The direct cyclic-sums viewer evaluates each frequency separately.

The denominator coupling at cutoff X = 7 includes q = 5 and 7. In general it uses each full row cq(1) = μ(q) once for X/2 < q ≤ X and gcd(q, 6) = 1. The optional χ₄ grading multiplies each row by +1 for q ≡ 1 mod 4 or −1 for q ≡ 3 mod 4. At X = 7 these sums are −2 and 0, respectively. The bound Oε(X1/2+ε) for every ε > 0 and all sufficiently large cutoffs remains open. Trace the unit-orbit identity in the proof atlas.
