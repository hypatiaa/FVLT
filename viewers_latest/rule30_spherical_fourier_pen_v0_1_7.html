The pen

The library's FVLTTurnFourierPenV1.pen (Lean, in the Rule 30 theorem library) is

pen(n) = Σt<n ct · ζPk·t,   ζP = e2πi/P.

As a pen: walk a step of length ct along the current heading, then turn by the fixed root of unity ζPk, k/P of a turn. The signal is the step; the turn is fixed. That is the opposite of the turtle pens, where the signal is the turn. Because the signal multiplies a root-of-unity character, pen(n) is a character sum, the shape of a Gauss sum. With ct = (t/P), the Legendre symbol, and P prime, pen(P) is exactly the classical quadratic Gauss sum g(k), and |g(k)|² = P for k ≢ 0. Blue dots mark the pen at whole turns n = P, 2P, 3P, …. The library proves that a signal is eventually periodic exactly when the steps between these dots eventually become constant (eventually_periodic_iff_uniform_constant_steps, boolean_period_iff_uniform_spin_steps).

On the sphere the same rule runs as a pen on S²: walk the arc ct·α along the heading (backwards for −1, not at all for 0), then turn by ζPk. The step α is itself a root of unity, ζ384a, a/384 of a turn. Each step is a rotation of the sphere, so the pen's state after n steps is an ordered product of rotations rather than a sum. The order now matters: the sphere pen is the non-commutative version of the character sum. As α → 0 it flattens back to the plane: near the start point the sphere path, divided by α, approaches pen(n). The card reports how close it is at the current α.

Watching the pen walk (v0.1.2)

Play moves the pen smoothly along each step, from one step every five seconds up to twenty steps a second; it starts at one step a second, and − Slower and Faster + change it while it walks. While it walks, the whole path is drawn faintly, the part already walked is highlighted, the last six steps are bold, and the pen is ringed; in the plane the scale is held fixed so the picture does not zoom. A step of length 0 (a white cell in the bit signal) is a turn only, so the pen pauses in place for that step. Pause, Step ▸ and Restart work as in the turtle pens. This is still the FVLT Fourier pen: the signal sets the step and the turn is the fixed root of unity ζPk.

Full screen (this version)

Every view has a ⛶ Full screen button in its top corner. In full screen the view fills the screen with its own controls: − Slower, Play, Faster +, Step ▸, Restart and the current speed, plus camera controls. Spheres turn with ⟲ ⟳ ▲ ▼ or by dragging; every view moves with ◀ ▶ ▲ ▼ or a two-finger drag (flat views also by a one-finger drag); every view zooms with − and +, a two-finger pinch, or the mouse wheel. Reset camera returns the view to its start, and ✕ Exit (or Escape) leaves full screen.

{p,q} skeletons (v0.1.4)

The sphere step can be set from a regular tiling {p,q}: cos(ℓ/2) = Re ζ2p / Im ζ2q, the same formula as the curvature pen. The faint lines are that tiling's skeleton, and the line under the selector says whether the Fourier pen stays on it.

Why only even q works. The Fourier pen's turn is fixed: it does not depend on the signal. After an edge the pen faces straight on; a fixed turn then has to point along an edge at every vertex, and a step of −1 (or a pause, for the bit signal) has to as well. At a vertex the q edges are 1/q of a turn apart, and "straight on" is half a turn from the edge just walked. Half a turn is a whole number of 1/q turns exactly when q is even. So for even q (the octahedron {3,4}, and the degenerate hosohedra and dihedra) the pen with turn ζq stays on the skeleton for every signal. For odd q (tetrahedron, cube, dodecahedron, icosahedron) no fixed turn can: those are offered to show the pen leaving. The turtle pens reach odd q because their turn follows the sign. This is a real difference between the two kinds of pen.

Export a run (fvlt-pen-run/0.1)

Every view has a ⇩ Run JSON button, on the page and in full screen. It saves the run as it is now: the stream (signs, seed, tooth events), the rule and its exact labels, the geometry (skeleton, lattice or tiling patch), the path for every step, the camera, the play speed, and statistics for every step (vertices, edges, tooth events, distance) with final totals. The renderer pen_tools/render_pen_run_v0_1.py turns such a file into a video like the Platonic-solid videos.
