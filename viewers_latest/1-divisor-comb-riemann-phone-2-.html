Spectral gearbox

This box asks: does the current shell N contain an evenly spaced cycle of m positions? It connects that divisibility check to the reference cycle drawn on the sphere.

How to read this box and use its buttons

Two numbers, two different questions. N is the current shell size. m is the number of teeth in your comb, and the size of the reference cycle. “Order m is prime” describes m. The separate “Wilson readout for shell N” describes N.

Resident means “fits exactly.” When m divides N with no remainder, you can choose m evenly spaced positions around an N-position circle. Their spacing is N/m, called the stride. With m = 2 and N = 700, the two positions are 350 apart. This is why your screenshot has an amber cycle.

Gear g moves through shell sizes. g is the same control as φ₃, and N = B + 1 − g. Increasing g by one decreases N by one. The first resident gear is g₀ = (B + 1) mod m; later resident gears are m steps apart, while N remains positive.

Order spectrum describes the cycle size m. φ(m), Euler’s totient, counts the step sizes from 1 to m − 1 that visit every position of an m-cycle before returning. The defect, m − 1 − φ(m), counts the remaining step sizes. For m ≥ 2, defect zero means m is prime. This φ(m) is a counting function; it is different from the shell control φ₃.

The buttons:

  • Previous resident / Next resident jump backward or forward in g to a shell divisible by m. “Next” increases g and decreases N.
  • m = n₁ + 1 sets the tooth count one above the selected factorial index. It does not change the base mode.
  • g = 0 returns to N = B + 1.
  • Show amber resident cycle toggles the divisibility reference drawing. The blue walk is unchanged.

Why Wilson belongs here. Choose a factorial base B = n₁!, then set m = n₁ + 1 and g = 0. Now N = (m − 1)! + 1. Wilson’s theorem says m divides this N exactly when m is prime. In that setting, a resident cycle is green and witnesses primality of m.

For example: choose 4!, press m = n₁ + 1, then g = 0. You get m = 5 and N = 25. The green cycle witnesses that 5 is prime; 25 remains composite.

What the picture shows. Amber means an exact divisibility relation; green means the special Wilson relation also holds. The overlay is a regular reference cycle, projected onto the sphere. The blue path is the actual comb walk, which also depends on spin, carrier and spread. The reference cycle does not assert that the blue walk closes or that all its teeth coincide.
