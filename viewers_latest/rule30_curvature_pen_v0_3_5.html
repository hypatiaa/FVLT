The general formula

In the regular tiling {p,q}, every face has p edges and q edges meet at every vertex. The q edges at a vertex are spaced 1/q of a turn apart, so a pen arriving along one edge finds the next edge to its left or right after a turn of (q − 2)/(2q) of a turn: the root of unity ζ2qq−2, or its inverse. The edge length ℓ is fixed by

cos(ℓ/2) = Re ζ2p / Im ζ2q

(on the sphere; in the hyperbolic plane cos becomes cosh). Whether the ratio is below, equal to, or above 1 is the same as whether pq is below, equal to, or above 2(p + q), that is, whether 1/p + 1/q is above, equal to, or below 1/2. That integer comparison picks the surface: sphere (k = +1), plane (k = 0), or hyperbolic plane (k = −1).

What is drawn

The stream gives one sign per step: for Rule 30 and Rule 150 it is the squeezed-sphere readout st = σ(xt(0) ⊕ xt(1)) of the centre pair from a single black cell; for the coin it is a fair ±1. The same signs drive all three panels. Each panel shows its tiling dimmed and the pen's path over it.

Sphere. The pen is run as a finite flag machine: every (vertex, heading) state the two moves can reach is enumerated once, and the path is read from that table, so it cannot drift. The card reports vertices, edges and states reached. Plane. The pen's position is an exact sum of roots of unity: Gaussian integers for squares (q = 4), Eisenstein integers for hexagons and triangles (q = 3, 6). Disk. The pen is a product of SU(1,1) matrices; the view follows the pen and the tiling patch is the one seen from the pen's own flag, so one patch serves every step.

Playback. Play walks the pen smoothly along each edge, from one step every five seconds up to twenty a second; − Slower and Faster + change the speed while it walks. Older steps are dimmed, the last six are bold, the pen is ringed. Every view has ⛶ Full screen with its own playback and camera controls.

Boolean cubes (v0.3.5)

A squarefree number with ω primes has the ω-cube as its divisor lattice: a divisor is a set of primes, and an edge adds or removes one prime. Put the primes on seats around a circle and keep only the squares whose two primes sit on neighbouring seats: that is the skin, a closed surface with 2ω vertices, ω·2ω−1 edges and ω·2ω−2 squares (the atom skin pen's surface). Four squares meet at nothing; ω squares meet at every vertex, so the skin is a {4,ω} map and the {4,ω} tiling in the other cards is its universal cover: the cube for Div(30) (sphere), a torus for Div(210) (plane), genus 5 for Div(2310) and genus 17 for Div(30030) (disk). The fourth card walks the skin with the same signs (atom skin pen rule: take the edge next to the back edge, side set by the sign) and draws it in the helical atlas layout of the atlas viewer: a divisor sits at minus the sum of its primes' seat directions, at height Ω, with a dashed ring per Ω layer. Points marked ×k carry k divisors (in Div(210) the divisors 10 and 21; in Div(30030) for example 14, 33 and 65). Degenerate case, labelled: Div(6) is one square whose vertices have two edges, so the pen circles the square whatever the stream. It has no tiling on the q dial.

Export a run (fvlt-pen-run/0.1)

Every view has a ⇩ Run JSON button, on the page and in full screen. It saves the run as it is now: the stream (signs, seed, tooth events), the rule and its exact labels, the geometry (skeleton, lattice or tiling patch), the path for every step, the camera, the play speed, and statistics for every step (vertices, edges, tooth events, distance) with final totals. The renderer pen_tools/render_pen_run_v0_1.py turns such a file into a video like the Platonic-solid videos.
