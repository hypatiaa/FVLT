FVLT orbit viewer v0.3

The FVLT Fourier pen as an orbit. Pick a turn on the rim; the signal supplies the steps; the pen walks ct along its heading and turns by the fixed root of unity ζPk. Sampled at complete turns it is the FVLT's native orbit. Everything printed is exact; everything drawn is placed by guaranteed intervals.

1The turn on the rim

tap or drag a bead to choose the turn k

Beads are the P-th roots of unity ζPj, coloured by their order d = P/gcd(j, P): one colour per divisor of P, the Div(P) classes. P runs from 2 to 384.

2The signal

Streams: Rule 30 / Rule 150 from one black cell, the centre cell xt(0) (caRun of pen-streams-core.js, v0.4.19, copied unchanged); bit is 0/1 and spin is ±1 with true = +1 (Lean bitSignal, spinSignal). Rule 150’s centre column is identically 1: from one black cell, xt(0) is the centre coefficient of (1 + x + x²)t mod 2, the central trinomial coefficient, and these are all odd (classical; checked exactly here for t < 4,096). So the Rule 150 bit and spin signals equal the constant signal 1. Squeezed centre-pair sign (new in v0.2, the surface pens’ readout): st = −1 iff xt(0) ⊕ xt(1) = 1, else +1, for Rule 30 and Rule 150 (caRun of pen-streams-core.js, unchanged). Coin: xorshift32 with shifts 13, 17, 5, +1 on an odd state (coinSigns). Goldbach: +1 for a kept tooth, −1 for a ghosted one, in v0.4.19's both-sides order c = 0, 1, −1, 2, −2, … (goldbachShell). Möbius and the divisor indicator are indexed by t + 1, so t = 0 reads μ(1) and 1d | 1. Every value is an integer.

3The pen, step by step

pen(n) = Σt<n ct ζPkt

Exact in ℤ[ζP]: the residue vector ar = Σt<n, kt ≡ r (mod P) ct (the library's residue form, step_eq_residue_sum), and its canonical form modulo the cyclotomic polynomial ΦP (coefficients of 1, ζ, …, ζφ(P)−1). With the constant signal the pen closes into the star polygon {P/g : k/g} after P/g steps (kernel Rmk 9.3i C5, the star-polygon reading, interpretive). With a real signal it is the partial Fourier sum at frequency k/P. Lean: FVLTTurnFourierPenV1.pen. |pen(n)|² is printed as an integer when it is rational; for prime P it usually is not.

4The return orbit: the native orbit

pen(P·x), x = 0, 1, 2, … (turn-sampled)
step dial: step(c, k, x) = pen(P(x+1)) − pen(Px)

Each step is exactly the DFT of the x-th block of P signal values at frequency k (Lean step_eq_plusDFT). The signal is eventually P-periodic iff these steps are eventually constant for every k (eventually_periodic_iff_uniform_constant_steps; Boolean forms boolean_period_iff_uniform_bit_steps, boolean_period_iff_uniform_spin_steps). The readout tests the steps for every k exactly in ℤ[ζP] (two blocks equal entry by entry give equal steps; otherwise a witness k with a non-zero difference is shown). It reaches only to T: a finite run shows the criterion's pattern but proves no periodicity, and a finite run settles nothing beyond T. Threshold: constant steps count only when they hold over at least 4 adjacent step pairs at the end of the run (each pair compares two complete turns, so this is more than two full turns’ worth of pairs); a shorter equal tail reads “constant only on a short tail (L pairs) at the end of the run: not settled within T”. Dots go from purple (early x) to gold (late x).

5Galois and conjugation orbits of the turn

Galois orbit: the φ(d) pens of order d, and their sum
conjugation: penk and the mirror pen−k
Div(P), with the order d of the turn

The turns a·k, a a unit mod P, give φ(d) pens of the same order d = P/gcd(k, P); they are the turns a·(P/d) with a a unit mod d. Their sum is Σt<n ct cd(t) with the Ramanujan sum cd(t) = μ(d/g)φ(d)/φ(d/g), g = gcd(t, d) (Hölder; t from 0, gcd(0, d) = d): the Ramanujan-channel readout of the signal at conductor d (kernel II.2; Ramanujan, Hölder and Kluyver are classical). It is an integer and its pen runs along the real axis (gold). The turn −k gives the mirror pen: σ−1, ζ ↦ ζ−1, is complex conjugation under ζP = e2πi/P (kernel II.23.5a), and for a real signal pen−k(n) = σ−1(penk(n)), checked exactly on the residue vectors.

6Rim placement: the flat Ford panel, the rolled-up disk and the cardioid

flat Ford panel: the upper half plane, 1/0’s bubble, ρ and i
rolled-up Ford disk: a/b on the rim at angle 2π·a/b
cardioid boundary λ ↦ λ/2 − λ²/4, |λ| = 1

The Fourier pen turns on the rim, where the Mandelbrot multiplier only arrives in the limit.

The rolled-up disk is the Fraction Fan's picture: τ in the upper half plane goes to e2πiτ, so the fraction a/b sits on the rim at angle 2π·a/b and its Ford bubble (centre a/b + i/(2b²), radius 1/(2b²)) becomes a loop touching the rim there. The fan is the geodesics from a/b to its Farey neighbours c/d (|ad − bc| = 1) with d up to the dial, and for b = 1 the line to infinity, which rolls up to the spoke to the centre. The cardioid panel draws only the boundary curve c = λ/2 − λ²/4 for |λ| = 1 and marks the bulb root at λ = ζba; it does not iterate z² + c and does not draw the Mandelbrot set. Ford circles and Farey fractions are classical.

The flat panel (new in v0.2). saphir asked what goes in the gap above the kiss of the 0 and 1 bubbles. The answer is 1/0’s bubble: the line Im τ = 1, a horocycle at infinity, tangent to the bubble of 0 at i and to the bubble of 1 at 1 + i. With the sides Re τ = 0, Re τ = 1 and the arc from 0 to 1 it bounds the Farey triangle (0, 1, 1/0); ρ = 1/2 + i√3/2 sits at its centre (order 3) and i at the kiss of 0’s bubble and the line (order 2). Tap the panel, or move the mouse over it, to set τ (v0.3: in each coordinate τ is the simplest fraction of the pixel’s exact cell, so τ and every orbit point are exact in ℚ(i)). In the gap (0, 1, 1/0), at the rim turn 1/3 the orbit is τ, R(τ), R²(τ) under R(z) = 1/(1 − z) (R sends 0 → 1 → 1/0 → 0, fixes ρ and closes after exactly 3 steps); at 2/3 it is the orbit under R⁻¹; at 1/2 it is τ, S(τ) under S(z) = −1/z, which fixes i and closes after 2 steps. For any other turn a/b with b ≥ 4 the panel says: PSL(2, ℤ) has rotations of order 2 and 3 only; this turn has no Ford-picture rotation. The Fourier pen still closes after b steps on the rim (classical). In the rolled-up disk, ρ goes to q = e2πiρ = −e−π√3 and i to e−2π, both next to the centre. Ford, Farey and the modular group are classical.

Every gap (new in v0.3). Move a finger or the mouse over the flat panel. The gap under τ lights up: its Farey triangle (p, m, q) is found exactly by descending the Stern–Brocot tree in rational arithmetic (a point on an edge belongs to the triangle above it; a point on a vertical Re τ = k belongs to the strip to its right). With M = [[c, a], [d, b]] (p = a/b, q = c/d, m the mediant) the gap is M of the gap (0, 1, 1/0), its centre is M(ρ), and the order-3 rotation about it is M R M⁻¹, which sends p → m → q → p. The kiss of two bubbles is the image M(i) of i on a Farey edge, and the half turn about it is M S M⁻¹; the order-2 orbit uses the kiss nearest to τ (hyperbolic distance) among the triangle’s three edges. The orbit choice “follow the rim turn” keeps v0.2’s rule (1/3: R, 2/3: R⁻¹, 1/2: S, otherwise the guard); “gap, order 3” and “kiss, order 2” work at every turn. The readout gives the gap’s corners, the integer rotation matrix and the orbit points exactly, and the bubble under τ, decided exactly. The flow: the dot moves along τ’s hyperbolic circle about the centre and passes through the orbit points. That circle is a Euclidean circle with centre (Re C, h) and radius² h² − (Im C)², where h = Im C · cosh r and cosh r = 1 + |τ − C|²/(2 Im τ Im C), exact (rational for a kiss, a rational multiple of √3 for a gap centre); it is drawn by intervals, and the moving dot comes from the interval cos and sin of rational turns. The flow respects “reduce motion”. Labels: every fraction with denominator up to the label dial (3 … 16, more when zoomed in) is written below the axis in up to five rows, and on the rolled-up disk on the rim in up to three rings; a label that would overlap another is skipped and counted. Fractions outside [0, 1] are written as mixed fractions (1 1/3, −1 1/4). Pan and zoom: ◀ ▶ move by a quarter of the width, − + zoom by 2. The picture repeats under z ↦ z + 1 (the translation T of the modular group), so every strip (k, k + 1, 1/0) looks the same. Ford, Farey, Stern–Brocot and the modular group are classical. Idea and questions: saphir.

6bThe Farey pen

tap a gap to start the pen there (same view as the flat panel)

The pen is built from the two moves of the gaps: S crosses the kiss ahead into the next gap, and R or R⁻¹ turns a third of a turn about the new centre, chosen by the signal’s sign. Its state is an exact matrix M of SL(2, ℤ), and the pen sits at M(ρ). Each value s = ±1 moves M ← M·S·Rs; a value 0 pauses the pen (no move, written ·). While the sign stays the same, the pen circles one bubble: for s = +1 the bubble of (M·S)(0) = M(1/0), for s = −1 the bubble of (M·S)(1/0) = M(0), with M the frame before the step. Checked exactly: constant +1 gives S·R = [[1, −1], [0, 1]] after one step, the translation z ↦ z − 1, and circles 1/0’s bubble; constant −1 circles the bubble of 0. Classical: the run lengths are the continued-fraction digits of where the pen heads. From the gap of ρ, runs a₁, a₂, … circle the bubbles of −[a₁], −[a₁; a₂], … (or −[0; a₁, …] when the first run is −1): the cutting-sequence reading of the Farey tessellation (C. Series, “The modular surface and continued fractions”, 1985). The page claims nothing beyond this. Signals: those of section 2 read by their sign (bit signals pause at 0), constant −1, and Legendre (t/p) for t = 0, 1, …, T − 1, the Gauss-sum signal (Σt<p (t/p) ζpt is the Gauss sum; t ≡ 0 gives 0, a pause). Farey, Ford, Gauss and the modular group are classical; the pen idea and the questions are saphir’s.

7About

Registers. Exact: BigInt integers and rationals; elements of ℤ[ζP] as residue vectors, compared by their canonical forms modulo ΦP; orders, φ, μ, Ramanujan sums; |pen(n)|² for prime P as an integer when it is rational; on the flat Ford panel, rationals, points of ℚ(i) and of ℚ(√−3), and integer matrices of PSL(2, ℤ). Guaranteed interval: every drawn position is a BigInt enclosure at scale 2−48 built from the interval cos, sin and π of pen-interval-core.js (v0.4.19, copied unchanged), and each panel prints how many enclosures are wider than ¼ pixel (should be 0) and how many straddle a pixel edge (pixel undecided). Drawing: integer pixels only. No floating-point arithmetic is used for the mathematics; the one non-integer input is the pointer position the browser reports, cut to an integer pixel on arrival.

New in v0.3: fraction labels on the flat panel and on the rolled-up disk (mixed fractions outside [0, 1]); the bubble under the pointer; hover or drag in every gap with its order-3 or order-2 orbit and the continuous flow; the Farey pen (6b); pan and zoom; legends clear of the lines; labels kept inside their panels.

New in v0.2: the flat Ford panel with 1/0’s bubble, ρ, i and the elliptic orbits (section 6); the short-tail status of the return readout (section 4); the squeezed centre-pair signals and the Rule 150 note (section 2); text readouts that follow a drag; dots drawn on the same pixel convention as the lines.

Not in v0.3 (as in v0.1 and v0.2): the spherical Fourier pen, which needs an exact rotation field; the lifted torus orbits of II.23.9; the Cm profile orbits.

Degenerate cases are drawn and labelled: k = 0 (the DC pen walks straight and each step is the block sum); T = 0 (nothing to draw); P = 1 is refused; an all-zero signal (the pen does not move).
