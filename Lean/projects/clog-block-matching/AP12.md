# Four Möbius progressions at square-root length

The published Theorem 1.1(i) implies the atlas proposition `AP12Measure`. The Lean package proves the specialization and a second reduction following the authors’ major-arc proof. Both reductions have explicit analytic hypotheses. An unconditional proof of `AP12Measure` in Lean is still pending.

## Direct use of Theorem 1.1(i)

Put (M=⌊X/2⌋) and (h=⌊√X⌋). Take the constant observable 1 on the trivial nilmanifold, fixed complexity, logarithmic exponent 1, and ε = 1/12. Equation (1.4) takes the supremum over arithmetic progressions contained in the real window. Its exceptional set is shared across progressions. The certificate `four_from_maximal` constructs each residue slice explicitly, proves it lies in the real window, and uses that same exceptional set. For every integer X ≥ 32 the length satisfies M^(5/12) ≤ h ≤ M^(11/12). A factor of two absorbs the change from log M to log X, including odd X. [Maximal-progression transfer](MaximalSlice.lean) · [Length, logarithms, and endpoints](DirectProgressions.lean) · [Theorem 1.1 and equation (1.4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05770v2#S1.Thmtheorem1)

## First missing input in the paper’s two-scale argument

Use T = 0 in Theorem 3.1(i), g = 1, and the zero Möbius approximant. Section 3.4 first supplies a pointwise long-interval estimate, immediately before (3.30), by citing Theorem 3.1 of Part I. Write

    U_r(x,H) = (1/H) ∑_{x<n≤x+H, n≡r (mod 12)} μ(n).

The first missing Lean lemma on this route is `LongWindow12`: there exist A > 0 and M₀ such that for every integer M ≥ M₀, every real x ∈ [M,2M], and r ∈ {1,5,7,11},

    |U_r(x,M^0.99)| ≤ A / log M.

The length M^0.99 is real. The Lean sum floors x and x+H separately. Ordinary dyadic summatory cancellation of size O(M/log^a M) for a fixed a would be too large after subtracting the two endpoints, since M^0.01 eventually exceeds every fixed power of log M. [Exact Lean proposition](ShortLong.lean#L12) · [Part I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03754) · [Part II, Section 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05770v2#S3.SS4)

The second missing input `ShortLong12` is the restricted variance estimate associated with (3.30). With the same M and h defined from X, it suffices to prove

    ∑_{r∈{1,5,7,11}} ∫_[M,2M] |U_r(x,h) − U_r(x,M^0.99)|² dx ≪ M / log³ M.

The authors compare a short mean with a long mean using Dirichlet polynomial estimates. We need the comparison only at the single length h and the four fixed classes. Lean proves that at any exceptional start at least one squared difference, multiplied by log² M, is ≥ 1. Integration gives the exact exceptional-measure conclusion. The proof handles measurability of the dyadic exceptional set and uses the existing atlas definition. [Variance and transfer](ShortLong.lean#L22) · [Paper’s comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05770v2#S3.E30)
